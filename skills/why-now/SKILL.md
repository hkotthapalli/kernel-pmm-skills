# Why now

The most common slide in venture and the least evidenced. This output treats the timing claim as a research question with a right answer, not a paragraph written last.

It is not only for fundraising. The chosen catalyst opens the deck (16), becomes the vision line in the messaging hierarchy (17), and opens the sales talk track (25). A company that never raises still needs it.

## The shape

A judgement output. One catalyst gets chosen and the rejected and struck claims stay on the page, so the reader sees what was considered and thrown out. Never present a single why-now with nothing beside it.

## What qualifies: a catalyst

A catalyst is **a dated external change you did not cause.** Five catalyst types count:

| Catalyst type | What it needs |
|---|---|
| **Regulatory change** | The rule, the date it took effect, any deadline |
| **Cost change** | The number crossed and when, so the business model works for the first time |
| **Behaviour change** | Third-party data with a date, never your own funnel |
| **Technology change** | A feasibility threshold crossed, never "the technology exists" |
| **Competitor change** | An acquisition, exit, shutdown or platform policy change, dated |

Not a catalyst:
- "The market is growing." A size claim. It belongs in 03.
- "We built something great." That is a differentiator.
- "Attitudes are shifting." Undated and unfalsifiable.
- "AI got good." Availability, not a threshold.
- A trend line going up. A gradient is not a moment.

## The three tests

1. **Two-year test.** Could this exact sentence have been said two years ago? If yes, strike it. Apply it first and honestly. A claim slightly less true two years ago still fails. Run it in reverse too: if it will be equally true for everyone in two years, it describes the world, not a window.
2. **Threshold test** (cost and technology changes only). Name the number crossed and the date. No number, strike it.
3. **Plan alignment check.** The market window must stay open past the plan's milestones: the launch date from 21 or 22, and the fundraise milestones from 16. If the window may close first, say so.

## Every claim needs a date and a verified source

No date, no catalyst. Record the evidence source on every line:
- **Verified:** checked against a primary source (statute, regulator, filing, official statistics) read in full.
- **Unverified:** your site, your deck or a belief. Unverified claims go to 05 Assumption log as timing assumptions, with what would verify them.

Regulatory dates are the most common place this output goes wrong. Verify each one against a primary source. If a date cannot be confirmed, drop the claim rather than approximating it.

## Collect before you choose

Collect every timing claim from the website, the deck, investor updates, 03 Market report (structural facts) and 07 The Fifty (competitor launches and exits), plus your own claims and any the research finds. Read 11 purchase triggers as context. Six to ten timing claims is normal. Rejected and struck claims are the proof the choice was made.

## Choose one

One catalyst. A slide with several weak reasons reads as hedging. For the chosen catalyst, state:
1. **Catalyst:** the event and its date
2. **What it enables:** the mechanism, in one sentence
3. **Market window:** when it opened, the **window close trigger** (a named event beats a duration), and a re-check date

If the claim has no close trigger, it is probably not a window. 10 Competitor monitor watches the close trigger.

## The why-now statement

Under 40 words, one catalyst, one consequence. It goes to 16, 17 and 25.

## Report structure

1. **Timing claims by date.** Every claim plotted at its catalyst date on an axis, undated claims in a dashed column.
2. **Tests.** Claim, catalyst type, date, evidence source, two-year, threshold, plan, result (Chosen, Rejected, Struck, To 05).
3. **Chosen catalyst.** Catalyst, what it enables, market window bar with the plan dates marked and a dashed end at the close trigger, re-check date.
4. **Unverified claims**, sent to 05.
5. **Key finding**, black band.
6. **Why-now statement**, hand-offs, Limitations.

## Drawing rules

- Time is the layout. Dates on an axis.
- The market window is a bar with an end, never an arrow off the page.
- Fill grammar: proposed thick outline, rejected light outline, unverified dashed, struck through.

## Language

Matches Kernel's PMM glossary.

| Use | Instead of |
|---|---|
| Why now | (stays) |
| Timing claims | The claims on the table |
| Catalyst | What changed, and when |
| Catalyst types: Regulatory change, Cost change, Behaviour change, Technology change, Competitor change | A rule changed, a cost changed |
| Two-year test | Could you have said this two years ago |
| Threshold test, Plan alignment check | (new) |
| Verified source, Unverified | (new) |
| Chosen catalyst, Rejected, Struck | The one we're making |
| What it enables | What it makes possible |
| Market window, Window close trigger | How long it holds, what closes it |
| Key finding, Limitations, Evidence source | The finding, what can't be known |

Never use "tailwind", "secular shift" or "macro trend". They mark slop.

## What the competition does

- **PitchKitchen** (read August 2026): a strategic narrative from a leadership workshop (old game, new game, stakes). No dates, sources, rejected claims or expiry.
- **Causo Hub guide** (read October 2026): five catalyst patterns, pick one, tie it to the ask. Advice, not a tool.
- **10x-why-now founder skill** (read October 2026): Thiel's question plus an adoption-curve check. No dates, sources or rejected claims shown.
- **Octave** seed post: an undated "Why now?" heading. An example of the slide this output replaces.

The gap: nobody ships a dated, sourced catalyst with the rejected claims visible and a watched close trigger. Do not overstate it. PitchKitchen has an artefact in this space.

## Standing rules for every output

- No invented findings. Every date must be checkable.
- No false zeroes. Check what exists before writing that nothing does.
- No competitor prices as structural facts.
- Name organisations, never individuals, when the work is confidential.
- "Not measured" is not an answer.
- Read the source, never the snippet. A search summary is a lead, not evidence.
- Tax, legal and regulatory claims carry a Limitations line telling you to check with an adviser before you publish.

## Where the method comes from

Sequoia's business plan template asks what has changed in technology, regulation or behaviour to make this the moment. The two-year test and the one-catalyst rule come from investor advice on these slides. None of this belongs in the output.
