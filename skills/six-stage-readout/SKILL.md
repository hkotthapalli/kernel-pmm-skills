# Six-stage readout

The capstone: one page that grades the whole product marketing system, quarterly. It is also the
engagement diagnostic — run at the start it is mostly dashed cells, and that drawing is the work
plan; run every quarter after, it is the accountability loop, and it grades the consultant's own
outputs as hard as anything else.

The market diagnoses everything except the artefacts. Pragmatic's gap analysis grades where time
goes. PMA's assessments grade team competency on invented maturity levels nobody can check. Starr
and Fletch use free diagnosis as the sales door. None of them grades the actual documents a
company runs on with questions that have checkable answers. This readout does only that.

## Three questions per output, all checkable

Every output in the six-stage map gets three cells:

1. **Does it exist?** A document someone can open. Not a plan to write one.
2. **Is it current?** Age against its half-life from the Half-Life Map, and event triggers beat
   dates: a competitor repricing stales a battlecard the same day.
3. **Was it used this quarter?** Read, copied from, or acted on, with one line of evidence.
   **An output nobody used is a finding, not a credit** — delivery is never reported alone.

No maturity levels. No scores out of five. Fill grammar only: solid = yes · half = partly ·
dashed = no or unknown · struck = checked and false (a document that exists but lies).

## The roll-up: one constraint

Grading 34 outputs produces a wall; the readout's value is the roll-up. Trace the dashed and
struck cells to their causes, and **name one constraint** — the thing blocking the most cells.

It is usually not a stage. It is usually a single unmade decision (a price, an unowned document,
an unsigned definition) that blocks cells across several stages at once. Naming the stage feels
strategic; naming the decision gives the founder something to do on Monday.

One constraint, never three. A readout that names three constraints has named none.

## Two deliverables

1. **The readout sheet** — the living one-pager: six stage bands, outputs as rows, three cells
   each, the constraint named at the bottom, quarter over quarter kept side by side after R2.
2. **The report** — the readout judged: the counts, the constraint argued from the cells, and
   what changed since last quarter.

For demo use, the report carries a miniature of the sheet.

## The two runs

- **Engagement start**: mostly dashed. That is the point — the first readout is the map of the
  work, priced and sequenced. Nothing sells the system like seeing it dark.
- **Quarterly after**: what lit up, what went stale, what was never used. The used column is the
  consultant's own report card; an engagement whose outputs are current but unused is failing in
  the way the 65%-unused statistic describes, and the readout says so.

## Guardrails

- **Every cell checkable.** If a cell cannot be verified by opening a document or asking one
  question, it does not belong on the sheet.
- **The consultant's outputs get no mercy.** Grading your own work soft once destroys the sheet's
  authority permanently.
- **Usage evidence is one line, not a survey.** "Sales copied the objection lines twice" is
  enough; instrumenting usage is a different project.
- **Existence is not progress.** A quarter that adds documents while the used column stays dashed
  is a bad quarter dressed as a good one.
- **Counts, never percentages under thirty. No invented findings. No false zeroes.**
- **The constraint must be traceable to cells.** If the named constraint does not explain the
  dashed cells, it is an opinion wearing a diagnosis.

## The report

Header bar (inverted): `Six-stage readout · R<N> · <quarter>`.

**01 · The system at a glance** — stat tiles, big mono numerals: outputs existing of total ·
current of existing · used this quarter of existing · cells blocked by the constraint.

**02 · The six stages** — the readout sheet drawn: six bands, each output a row of three cells in
fill grammar, stage subtotals in the band headers.

**03 · The finding** — the numbered black band, 25px, plain words.

**04 · The constraint, traced** — the named decision with the cells it blocks drawn as a chain
across stages; who owns it; what unblocks on the day it is made.

**05 · What changed** (from R2 onward) or **the work plan** (R1) — the sequence the dashed cells
imply, first ten items only.

**06 · What can't be known** — always including: the used column relies on people answering
honestly about their own neglect; one readout is a baseline, not a verdict; and a readout grades
documents, not the market — the funnel and trust readings say whether any of it works.

## Drawing rules

- The document shell: 1px black frame, inverted mono header bar, numbered eyebrows, hairline
  section rules, finding at 25px in the band.
- Six bands with BandRule separators; rows of three small cells; stage subtotals as mono
  fractions in band headers.
- The constraint chain drawn as a line touching every blocked cell.
- Fragments in drawings. Counts, never percentages under thirty.

## Language

| Use | Never |
|---|---|
| Does it exist, is it current, was it used | Maturity, capability, sophistication |
| The constraint | Key priorities, focus areas |
| Checked and false | Outdated (for a lying document) |
| The work plan the dashed cells imply | Roadmap, transformation journey |

## Standing rules for every output

- **Three cells per output, all checkable. One constraint, never three.**
- **Delivery never reported without usage.**
- **The readout reads; fixes belong to the outputs' own skills.**
- **Name organisations, never individuals**, when the work is confidential.
- **Sentence case. British spelling. No em dashes. No balanced two-part lines.** Written for a
  cold reader.
