# Sales enablement

The industry ships enablement as volume, and the data says it drowns: 65% of the content marketing makes for sales is never used (SiriusDecisions; Forrester puts it at 60 to 70%). The platforms (Highspot, Seismic, Showpad, Spekit) answer with repositories and training systems built for rep teams. At a company where the founder is the sales team, none of that fits.

This kit is deliberately small, built for the person actually in the call, and **usage is the measure**: every asset names its moment of use, and a usage log records when it was used. An asset with no moment of use does not ship.

## Two deliverables

**1. The sales enablement kit.** Four working assets on the client's own drive:

- A battlecard per real competitor, status quo first
- The objection handling library, capped at eight, with the one-page objection card
- The demo script, per buying role
- The sales playbook, one page per stage

**2. The report.** The kit judged: which assets rest on evidence today, which are unvalidated until real calls happen, and the key finding.

On a real engagement both ship; for demo or portfolio use, the report alone is enough.

## The evidence this rests on

- Klue's audit of 150+ battlecards: **19% carry supporting evidence; every battlecard with the highest retention carries it.** So every claim on a battlecard carries its grade from the Claim check.
- The founder-led sales canon (built from 300+ early-stage founders): the founder is the best seller early; sales calls are learning, not closing; outreach is relevance plus one counterintuitive insight plus the problem, in four sentences or fewer; no demo on the first call; book the next call on this call; qualification is the biggest available improvement; trust is the currency, and overselling costs more than the deal.
- Battlecard guides (Klue, Salesforce, Highspot) agree on one page, scannable, top objections first, a proof point on every response. Build landmines from real customer complaints, not guesses.

## Asset 1 · Battlecards

One page per competitor. A thirty-second reference, not a research document: the buyer says "we're also looking at X" and you need the right sentence within seconds.

**The first battlecard is against the competitor that actually wins.** Where Win / loss or the interviews show deals lost to the status quo (the buyer chose nothing, or kept the current solution), the status quo battlecard comes first.

Rules, non-negotiable:

- **Claim and response, always paired.** An unanswered competitor fact is ammunition for the other side. Three legitimate responses: not true (with evidence), true but irrelevant to this buyer, true and we do it too. Say which.
- **Every number carries a source and a date**, or is marked `Verify before use`. Every claim of ours carries its Claim check grade in fill grammar.
- **Concede real strengths, then reframe.** A card claiming the competitor has no strengths gets abandoned the first time a buyer proves otherwise.
- **The dependency check runs both directions** before anything is written: does anyone in the chain resell, license, embed or depend on the other's data or distribution. A dependency turns a feature argument into a structural one.
- **Landmine questions**: the question you ask that the competitor answers badly. Asking a question that reveals a weakness is selling; stating the weakness is marketing.
- **Where we lose, mandatory.** The two or three scenarios where the competitor is genuinely the better choice, drawn from the ICP disqualifiers. This is what makes the rest of the card believable, and it stops cycles burning on unwinnable deals.
- **A 30-second talk track**, readable aloud, in your voice.
- **Retire the battlecard when another company acquires the competitor** — the argument structure dies with the company, not just the facts.

Moment of use: the second a buyer names a competitor.

## Asset 2 · Objection handling library

Capped at eight. A thirty-objection document does not get read.

Evidence first: interviews, transcripts and loss reasons, then the objections you hear most and the ones you least like hearing — the second list surfaces more. **Never invent an objection**: anything not heard is marked `Unvalidated — needs five call reviews`.

The stated objection is rarely the real one. "Too expensive" is usually unclear value, the wrong buyer, or fear of the internal justification. Each entry carries five things: the objection verbatim, the root cause, **the diagnostic question asked before answering**, the reframe (change the axis of comparison, not a counter-argument), and the proof point with its grade. Responses under three sentences.

**Some objections are correct.** Write the honest answer: what is true, what compensates, and the walk-away criteria. A library that pretends every objection is a misunderstanding teaches you to fight every deal equally.

Each objection maps to a message pillar from the Messaging framework. An objection with no pillar is a missing pillar or a product gap; a pillar with no objection may be a pillar nobody cares about. Both go in the report and to the Messaging framework.

Ships in two forms: the full library, and the one-page objection card with just the objection and the two-sentence response.

Moment of use: the objection card stays open during the call; the library is for the night before.

## Asset 3 · Demo script

A story told with software, not a tour of it. Per buying role, because the same product shown to the end user, the champion and the economic buyer is three different demos — and the economic buyer or co-decision maker gets the shortest.

- Open on the outcome, never the login. Time to value under three minutes.
- The pain named in the buyer's words, from the language bank. A demo without discovery is a product tour with a founder attached.
- One reveal. Tell, show, tell.
- Realistic data. "Test Company 123" costs more credibility than a missing feature.
- "Can it do X?" has three honest answers: yes (show it briefly), yes but after this, and no — then ask why they need it. Never "on the roadmap" without a date you would put in writing.
- **No demo on the first call.** The first call is discovery. Demoing first avoids the questions the demo needed.

Moment of use: the second call, after discovery.

## Asset 4 · Sales playbook

One page per stage of the cycle: outreach, qualification, discovery, demo and proposal, then procurement (B2B) or first purchase (B2C). Not a forty-page playbook — one page each.

- **Outreach**: relevance to the role, one counterintuitive insight from the research, the problem not the solution, four sentences maximum. The insight comes from the Market report, Win / loss or Voice of customer, not from imagination.
- **Qualification**: the ICP disqualifiers, phrased as questions for the first five minutes.
- **Discovery**: the switch interview timeline from Voice of customer, reused.
- **The next call is booked on this call.** "I'll email you" is a polite no.
- **The scope is co-authored** with the buyer, and procurement is made frictionless: fill the forms, answer the questions, remove every reason to stall. For B2C, send the co-decision maker the asset from the Buying committee.

Moment of use: pinned wherever you work.

## Usage log

After each call, one line: the date, the assets used, the objection raised, whether the next call got booked. Each asset shows how many calls it appeared in. An objection moves from unvalidated to validated after five logged calls. New objections go to the FAQ by the twice-asked rule.

## The report's structure

Header: `Sales enablement · 4 assets · N of N claims graded · <date>`.

**01 · Sales enablement kit** — four glyph tiles, one per asset, each with its glyph (card, list, screen, page), the asset name, a short mono moment-of-use tag, and its evidence mark. No prose table.

**02 · Status quo battlecard** — the battlecard rendered as a miniature artefact: black header strip with the card name and how often it wins, claim and response pairs as fragments with grades, landmine question and where we lose as compartments, the 30-second talk track as the only full sentences in the section.

**03 · Objection handling library** — a stat row first (heard in interviews / from other outputs / unvalidated), then each objection as mark plus verbatim plus count plus pillar, then one full entry including walk-away criteria.

**04 · Demo script** — the time bar with the reveal marked, then per-role length bars (the shortest visibly shortest). One line of rules beneath.

**05 · Sales playbook** — one tile per stage.

**06 · Key finding** — one sentence, black band.

**07 · Limitations** — three short lines, always including: a kit built before real sales calls is a set of hypotheses, and the unvalidated marks are the honest state, not a fault.

## Drawing rules

- **Fragments, never sentences, in claim and response pairs.** "One month per year. Not invested." beats the paragraph it replaces.
- The battlecard is drawn as the artefact itself, small — the reader should recognise a card, not read a description of one.
- Each asset carries a glyph and a moment-of-use tag.
- The demo as a time bar, reveal marked, three minutes flagged; per-role bars beneath.
- Claim grades in fill grammar. Green only on Proven claims, the reveal, and assets used in a logged call.
- Counts, never percentages under thirty.
- The talk track is the one licensed piece of prose. Everything else in the drawings is a fragment or a mark.

## Standing rules for every output

- **Two deliverables: the kit and the report.**
- **Every asset names its moment of use, or it does not ship.** The 65% of enablement that goes unused was built without one.
- **Every claim carries its grade.** The kit consumes the Claim check; it never re-argues it.
- **Never invent an objection, a competitor fact, or a customer.**
- **Honesty is the mechanism, not the decoration**: where we lose, walk-away criteria and unvalidated marks are what make the rest believable.
- **Read the source, never the snippet.**
- **Name organisations, never individuals**, when the work is confidential.
- **Sentence case. British spelling. No em dashes. No balanced two-part lines.**
