# Onboarding journey

People sign up and disappear. Signups grow while daily actives flatline, and every number an investor asks about — daily and monthly active users, feature usage, revenue — sits downstream of a moment nobody defined. This output defines it, then builds the machine that moves people to it.

## The deliverables

Five, and only one is a document:

1. **The activation moment, signed.** One event with a window that means "this person got what they came for". Not signup, not login: the first time the outcome lands. It carries the founder's signature like every pass number in the system, because until it is decided, "active user" is a vibe. Benchmarks give a bearing without pretending precision: surveyed activation rates average 34%, median 25%, and vary hugely by product.
2. **The journey map, one page.** From the promise that recruited them, through the three moments — **set up · felt it work · came back on their own** — to paying. Each step: what must happen, by when, on which layer, and the event that proves it.
3. **The messages, actually written.** The welcome message itself, the day-2 nudge, the day-7 pull-back — copy ready to load, not instructions to write copy. On the channel the user actually opens: WhatsApp where the audience lives there, email, push only when earned.
4. **The in-product build list, handed to product.** Checklist items, empty states, do-this-next prompts, the demo video placed at the moment of confusion.
5. **The early-warning triggers with owners.** Each signal paired with a specific intervention and the person who fires it.

Plus the report that judges the set. On a real engagement everything ships; for demo or portfolio use, the report alone is enough.

## The rules

**The journey starts from the promise, not the product.** Message one's only job is making the recruiting surface's promise come true — a homepage that says two minutes owes the user those two minutes tonight. Every promise-to-reality gap is named in week one: disclosed then it is a plan, discovered in week three it is a trust problem.

**Guidance at the moment of need, never a tour.** A nine-step product tour on day one gets skipped; a prompt at the moment of confusion gets used. The in-product list is organised by moment, not by feature.

**Three layers, one journey:**

- **In-product** — the checklist, empty states, nudges, the video where confusion happens.
- **The drip** — sequenced to the three moments, on the channel the user opens.
- **The AI layer, where it earns its place** — conversational intake instead of a form; an agent that does the setup step for the user instead of pointing at it; the predictive catch, where a user matching the churn pattern triggers a human before they vanish. Listed only where the product can actually do it; an AI layer marked TBD is a build decision, not a message.

**One stream per person, per route, never merged.** The consumer user and the partner who decides with them. On an employer route: the employee, the HR champion, the budget owner. **The champion gets a forwardable progress note at day 30 and day 90** — something they can send their boss unedited. It does more for renewal than any nurture sequence.

**The metrics tree drawn explicitly.** Activation feeds active-user numbers; active users feed feature adoption; adoption feeds money. Instrument each moment, not just signup. The Channel and funnel output consumes this activation moment for its "started" stage and never redefines it.

**Never market to someone who just said yes.** They already bought the promise; the job now is delivery. Onboarding activates; nurture persuades; the two never share a sequence. Logins are not value.

**Timings follow real time-to-value.** A 30-day arc copied onto a product that takes a quarter produces messages that arrive before they matter and train the user to ignore everything.

## Early warnings

Flagged by day 30, not day 300, each with an owner and a play: no activation · one session then silence · single user on a multi-user account · the champion gone quiet · basics questions in week four. On a consumer product the churn-pattern catch is the AI layer's best job.

## The report's structure

Header: `Onboarding journey · <activation moment> · N messages written · <date>`.

**01 · The moment** — the activation event drawn large with its window, signed or awaiting signature, and the metrics tree it feeds: activation → active users → adoption → money.

**02 · The map** — the three moments as a horizontal journey, each step with its layer tag (in-product / drip / AI), its channel, and its proving event. Dashed where the product cannot do it yet.

**03 · The streams** — per route, per person, the message list with written/not-written marks and the champion's forwardable note called out.

**04 · The build list and the watch list** — what product must build, and the early-warning triggers with owners.

**05 · The finding** — one sentence, black band.

**06 · What can't be known** — always including: the activation moment is a bet until cohorts confirm it predicts retention; message copy decays as the product moves; and a journey nobody instruments is a hope.

## Drawing rules

- The journey as one horizontal band with the three moments as heavy marks; steps beneath with layer tags.
- The metrics tree as a chain of four linked marks, activation first.
- Written/not-written and can/cannot-do-yet in fill grammar.
- Counts per route, never merged. No percentages under thirty.

## Language

| Use | Never |
|---|---|
| Set up, felt it work, came back on their own | Setup moment, aha moment, habit moment |
| The moment they got what they came for | Activation event, north star input |
| The forwardable note | Executive summary, QBR readout |
| Came back on their own | Retained, resurrected |
| The churn-pattern catch | Predictive intervention engine |

## Standing rules for every output

- **The activation moment carries a signature.** Unsigned is recorded, not replaced.
- **Message one honours the promise that recruited them.**
- **Copy is written, not described.**
- **One stream per person, per route.**
- **No invented findings. No false zeroes.** A layer the product cannot run yet is dashed, not omitted.
- **Read the source, never the snippet.**
- **Name organisations, never individuals**, when the work is confidential.
- **Sentence case. British spelling. No em dashes. No balanced two-part lines.** Written for a cold reader.
