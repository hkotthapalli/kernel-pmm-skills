# Launch post-mortem

Most launch retros are a meeting where everyone says it went well and nothing changes. The market
guarantees this: no agency on the competitive list sells a retro, and every agency case-study page
is the anti-retro — curated wins, no misses. The honest methods come from outside marketing. The
US Army's after-action review asks four questions. Google's SRE practice adds the blameless
mechanics. This output is those two methods pointed at a launch.

It exists to produce three things: a verdict against a number agreed before launch, one named
diagnosis, and changes that edit named documents. Run it 30 to 90 days after launch — early enough
that people remember, late enough that the numbers mean something.

## The precondition

**The pass number from the Launch brief, unchanged.** Never renegotiate the target after seeing
the result; that is the single most common way a retro becomes theatre.

If no number was agreed, that is the top line of the whole retro: *"No success number was set
before launch, so the launch cannot be judged against intent. The most valuable change from this
retro is that the next launch gets its number first."* That sentence is worth more than any
analysis substituted for it. Then read the numbers anyway — honestly, with the caveat standing.

## The four questions (the AAR spine)

**1 · What was supposed to happen.** Read from the Launch brief: the pass number, the audiences,
what each was supposed to come to believe, the checklist the tier fired. Read, never reconstructed
from memory — memory moves goalposts.

**2 · What happened.** Numbers against a baseline: the prior period, the prior comparable launch,
or the run rate before. A number without a baseline is decoration. Read at week 4 to 8, not week
1 — the launch spike decays and week one flatters every launch. Absolute numbers beside every
percentage; never a percentage under 30 people.

**3 · Why the difference.** Locate the drop in the funnel; the stage of the drop names the failure:

| The right people... | Failure type | Whose problem |
|---|---|---|
| never heard | Awareness | Channel, reach, timing |
| heard and didn't care | Message | Positioning |
| cared and didn't act | Conversion | Friction, price, proof |
| acted and it didn't deliver | Product | Not marketing's — say so |
| acted, asked, and the answer was poor | Readiness | The internal launch was skipped |

Name one primary failure. A retro that names all five has named none. The product/launch
separation is the critical judgment: a well-executed launch of a product nobody wants is a
successful launch and a failed product, and the two fixes are unrelated.

**4 · Sustain and improve.** Two or three things that clearly worked, promoted to defaults. Then
the changes — and **every change is written as an edit to a named document**: the tier questions,
the checklist, the brief template, the messaging matrix, the enablement gate. A change that does
not edit a document does not survive to the next launch.

## Skipped items, costed

Pull the tier checklist and list everything skipped or never completed: what, why, and whether the
evidence says it mattered. This section recalibrates the tier framework — an item skipped three
launches running with no cost is not a requirement; an item whose skipping shows up as a readiness
failure becomes a gate.

## Blameless mechanics (the SRE rules)

- Facts and artefacts, never individuals. "Enablement was not completed before launch" is a fact;
  "the team could have communicated better" is fog. No blame, but no fog either.
- Circulated to everyone named in the launch brief, including those whose work is criticised.
  Retros distributed only upward become PR.
- Launch-period revenue is not launch-caused revenue. Say "coincided with" unless the causal read
  is real.
- If it went well, say so plainly and move to what to repeat. Manufacturing problems to look
  rigorous is its own failure mode.

## Two deliverables

1. **The retro record** — two pages: verdict table, the four questions answered, skipped items
   costed, changes as an owned edit list with the document each one edits. Circulated per the
   blameless rules.
2. **The report** — the judgment on the launch and on the process, for the founder.

For demo use, the report carries a miniature of the record.

## The report

Header: `Launch post-mortem · <launch> · week <N> read`.

**01 · The verdict** — target vs actual, hit / missed / cannot be judged. If no number was set,
the black band carries that sentence and everything below reads under it.

**02 · What the numbers did** — the funnel drawn with the drop marked, baseline beside each stage.

**03 · The diagnosis** — the five failure types as a row of tiles, the named one solid, the rest
dashed; two lines of evidence under the named one.

**04 · Skipped and costed** — checklist items as struck marks with the cost line each earned.

**05 · Sustain / improve** — two columns: what becomes default; each change with the document it
edits, in strikethrough-rewrite form where it edits copy.

**06 · What can't be known** — always including: a retro without a pre-set number reads history,
not performance; week-4-to-8 numbers on a small base stay small; and causation between launch
activity and revenue is asserted, not shown, at this scale.

## Drawing rules

- The verdict as a table with fill-grammar marks, not prose.
- The funnel with the drop visibly located — the diagnosis should be legible before the text.
- Failure-type tiles: one solid, four dashed. Skipped items struck.
- Fragments in drawings; sentences in the record. Counts, never percentages under thirty.

## Language

| Use | Never |
|---|---|
| What was supposed to happen | Objectives review, OKR retrospective |
| The launch or the product | Marketing performance |
| Cannot be judged against intent | Failed, when no number was set |
| Edits the checklist | Learnings, takeaways |

## Standing rules for every output

- **The number is read from the brief and never renegotiated.**
- **One primary failure named.** The drop's stage names it.
- **Every change edits a named document.**
- **No invented findings. No false zeroes. Baselines beside every number.**
- **Name organisations and artefacts, never individuals.**
- **Sentence case. British spelling. No em dashes. No balanced two-part lines.** Written for a
  cold reader.
