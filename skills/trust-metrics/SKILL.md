# Trust metrics

For any company asking people to hand over money, data or their retirement, trust is not a soft
theme. It is the constraint, and it can be measured. This output measures it in three layers,
strongest evidence first, and reads them quarterly — trust moves slower than the funnel, so this
ledger reads four times a year where the funnel reads twelve.

Nobody on the competitive list sells this at founder scale. Wynter sells the panel version (brand
tracking against a research panel); this ledger is what a founder can run without one, and it says
plainly where the panel version begins.

## The three layers

### 1. Revealed — what people do. The strongest evidence.
Trust shown with behaviour, not words: they kept coming back · they brought someone (a referral is
trust transferred to a friend) · they connected real money or real data · and on the B2B route,
**they renewed against the baseline**. The renewal read is absorbed from the renewal method: quote
the baseline back in their words, show the year against it, include the friction. An account whose
value section cannot be filled is the loudest trust alarm there is.

### 2. Declared — what they say when asked.
One question, the Sean Ellis product-market fit question: *"How would you feel if you could no
longer use this?"* The line is drawn before the survey runs: 40% or more saying "very
disappointed" is fit. It asks about loss, not liking, so it dodges politeness.

**NPS is not used.** Keiningham's 2007 replication across insurance, airlines and ISPs found it
predicts growth no better than plain satisfaction questions. If the client already runs NPS, keep
it as a trend line and say what it is: a trend line, not a truth.

### 3. Borrowed — what others lend.
Trust imported from third parties: a named regulator · a stated custody arrangement · app-store
rating with its count · named customers **with permission on record** · vouches from voices the
audience trusts (Edelman 2026: 44% trust financial influencers; 57% of those would reconsider a
distrusted company a finfluencer vouches for).

**Every borrowed item chains to the Claim check.** Borrowed trust the company cannot back is not
an asset; it is a liability with a date on it.

## Two deliverables

1. **The trust ledger** — one page the founder reads quarterly: the three layers as rows of
   checkable items, each solid (measured, holds), half (partial), dashed (unmeasured), or struck
   (checked and false). The Ellis result and the 40% line. The renewal reads for B2B accounts.
2. **The report** — the ledger judged; on a first reading, the instrument list with the cheapest
   instrument named.

For demo use, the report carries a miniature of the ledger.

## The first reading

Like the funnel reading, run one honestly rather than waiting for instruments. Most cells will be
dashed. Each dashed cell gets an owner and a cost, and the report names the **cheapest
instrument** — usually the Ellis question, which costs one message to existing users and needs
roughly thirty answers to mean anything.

## Guardrails

- **Behaviour outranks answers; answers outrank badges.** A referral beats a survey; a survey
  beats a logo strip.
- **The 40% line is written before the survey goes out** and never moved after.
- **Counts beside every percentage; nothing under thirty answers.**
- **Borrowed trust is audited, not collected.** An unnamed regulator or an unpermissioned logo is
  a struck cell, not a blank.
- **Never survey the same people twice in a quarter.** Small bases burn fast.
- **The panel boundary stated plainly**: unaided recall at the moments people enter the category
  is real brand tracking, needs a paid panel, and the ledger does not pretend to reach it.

## The report

Header bar (inverted): `Trust metrics · reading N · <quarter>`.

**01 · The ledger at a glance** — stat tiles: items measured of total · the Ellis result or
"never asked" · borrowed items that survive the Claim check, of total.

**02 · The three layers** — rows of fill-grammar cells per layer, strongest first, each item a
fragment with its state and its instrument or owner.

**03 · The finding** — the numbered black band, 25px, in plain words a cold reader gets first
pass.

**04 · The cheapest instrument** — what to run this quarter, its cost, its pass line written
down.

**05 · What can't be known** — always including: behaviour needs the funnel's instruments before
this ledger can read it; one survey is a point, not a trend; and what the wider market thinks is
unknowable without a panel, and that is a purchase, not a failing.

## Drawing rules

- The document shell: 1px black frame, inverted mono header bar, numbered eyebrows, hairline
  section rules, finding at 25px in the band.
- Three layers as three bands, strongest at top; fill grammar throughout; big mono numerals for
  the stats.
- The 40% line drawn as a threshold mark before any result exists.
- Fragments in drawings. Counts, never percentages under thirty.

## Language

| Use | Never |
|---|---|
| What people do / what they say / what others lend | Behavioural, attitudinal, reputational |
| Trust transferred to a friend | Virality, referral coefficient |
| A trend line, not a truth | NPS as a KPI |
| Checked and false | Unverified |

## Standing rules for every output

- **Quarterly, not monthly.** Trust moves slowly; reading it too often reads noise.
- **Every borrowed item chains to the Claim check.**
- **No invented findings. No false zeroes. Pass lines written before tests run.**
- **Name organisations, never individuals**, when the work is confidential.
- **Sentence case. British spelling. No em dashes. No balanced two-part lines.** Written for a
  cold reader.
