# Market report

Answers one question: how big is this market, and can you prove it.

It does not cover who to sell to first (11), why this year (04), or the competition (07). Those are separate outputs.

## Two deliverables

**1. The sizing model.** The funnel as an editable spreadsheet on your own drive. One row per filter, and every assumption (income filter, move intent, penetration rate, ARPU or ACV) is its own labelled cell with live formulas. Each input carries its data quality (Sourced, Mixed, Assumed) and its source and date. When an investor challenges the penetration rate, you change one cell and show the result. The model is what gets updated when a new statistical release lands.

**2. The report.** The funnel drawn, TAM SAM SOM, sensitivity, the triangulation gap, the capacity check, structural facts, the key finding.

## Market definition first

Category, buyer type (B2B, B2C or both, from 11), buyer, and geography. Get scope wrong and the whole report is wrong.

Scope test: does the structural fact that creates this market hold outside the home country? If yes, TAM uses the region.

- **TAM:** the region
- **SAM:** the beachhead segment from 11, the part you can sell to today
- **SOM:** what you can win in 3 years, after the capacity check

## The rule

**Bottom-up sizing first. Top-down sizing second. Then show the triangulation gap.**

Always produce a bottom-up number. "Not measured" is not an answer. A number with visible assumptions can be argued with. A blank cannot.

## Bottom-up, B2B

1. **Write the filters.** Industry, company size, geography, and the purchase trigger. Specific enough that someone else could rebuild the list.
2. **Count them** from a primary source: registries, government statistics, filings, trade bodies, Sales Navigator, Crunchbase. Record where the count came from.
3. **Multiply by ACV.** Use the price point from 15. If there is none, anchor to 3 to 5 comparables and use the median.
4. **Apply a penetration rate.** Above 10% in year 1 needs a named comparable.

## Bottom-up, B2C

1. Start with the widest public population figure for the region.
2. Apply filters in order, showing the number after each: residency, working, age band, income or job type, then any behavioural filter. Filters come from 11.
3. Mark every filter **Sourced, Mixed or Assumed.** The income filter almost always decides the answer and is almost never published.
4. Multiply by ARPU (annual revenue per user, from 15).
5. Apply a penetration rate and justify it in one line.

## Sensitivity analysis

Rank every assumption by how far it moves SOM between a low and a high value. Show the top 3 as range bars, with the value editable. These are the assumptions that decide whether the number survives a challenge. Send each Assumed one to 05 Assumption log and name the test: 01 interviews for behaviour, the 15 Van Westendorp survey for income and price.

## Capacity check

SOM from penetration cannot exceed what your channels can reach in 3 years. Take channel capacity from 23 Channel and funnel, or mark it Assumed until 23 is done. Use the lower number.

## Draw the funnel, do not table it

Each filter is a bar drawn to scale with the number beside it. The final bar should be nearly invisible against the first. That drop is the argument. In the model the funnel is rows: same filters, same order, same labels.

## Top-down sizing

1. Take the deck's figure, or the published figure for the category.
2. Record the source, the date, and its **source tier**: primary statistics, filings, data platforms, analyst reports. Analyst and aggregator figures never become the headline.
3. Older than 24 months is **Stale**. Label it.
4. Note who published it and what they gain if it is large.
5. Say what it counts. Assets, spend and revenue are different markets.

## The triangulation gap

Both numbers as bars side by side. If the gap is too large to draw to scale, say "not to scale" and give the ratio.

**Be fair to your number.** If the deck's figure is right as a headcount or as assets and wrong as a market, say exactly that. Then say which number goes in the deck: usually SAM and SOM, with TAM beneath.

## Structural facts

True regardless of who is selling. They age slowly.

**Never claim a zero when something exists.** State what exists, then why it falls short. Check for what you left out: statutory schemes, employer benefits, tax wrappers, government programmes, informal options, the status quo.

Not structural facts: a competitor's price, a product feature, anything that changes when a competitor changes. That belongs in 07.

When a structural fact varies by country, draw it as a row of squares: solid applies fully, half partial or voluntary, dashed does not apply.

## Key finding

**The key finding is the gap, not the size.** "Thirty-three million people work here" is a headcount. "The money already exists and nobody invests it" is a market. One sentence.

## Report structure

1. **Bottom-up sizing**, drawn to scale, every filter with its data quality
2. **TAM, SAM, SOM**, nested
3. **Sensitivity analysis**, top 3 assumptions as range bars
4. **Triangulation gap**, top-down against bottom-up, with source tier and what it counts
5. **Capacity check**
6. **Structural facts**, by country where they vary
7. **Key finding**, black band
8. **Limitations**

## Sourcing

- Every figure carries a source and a date, or reads Assumed. Assumed is allowed. Unmarked is not.
- Never use a number you cannot recompute.
- Label company figures as company figures.
- **Read the source, never the snippet.** Open the statistical release, filing or report and read the table. A search result that says "the market is worth $X" is a lead. If a figure cannot be traced to a document read in full, mark it Assumed or drop it.
- **Do not project growth** unless the source projected it, and then say who.

## What the competition does

- **Causo Hub, AI for market sizing** (read October 2026): bottom-up with primary-source unit counts, ACV from comparables, explicit penetration, assumptions ranked by sensitivity, top-down only as a cross-check. Advice, not a tool.
- **Agent skills and sizing tools** (Growwstacks agent and several market-sizing skills, seen in search, not read in full): generate TAM, SAM and SOM with sensitivity analysis. None found ties filters to the ICP or judges the deck's figure fairly.

## Standing rules for every output

- Two deliverables: the sizing model and the report.
- No invented findings. No false zeroes.
- No competitor prices as structural facts.
- Name organisations, never individuals, when the work is confidential.
- "Not measured" is not an answer.
- Round to the precision the source supports.
