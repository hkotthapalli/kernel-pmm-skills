# Win / loss

Finds out why deals were really won and lost, by asking the buyer after they have already decided.

The output is not a list of loss reasons. It is the **reason gap: the recorded loss reason against the buyer's stated reason.** That gap is the product.

## The deliverables

Three, not one.

**1. The pre-call log.** One row per closed deal: the deal, the outcome, and the recorded loss reason as written down (or your assumption, labelled as such), captured in writing **before any interview happens** and then sealed. This is a deliverable, not a working note, because without it there is no comparison — and because on the next round of deals the client fills it in themselves as each deal closes.

**2. The interview guide.** The rules and the eight questions on one page, written so the client can commission or run the next round without you. It includes the warning about who should not conduct the interviews, with the bias numbers.

**3. The report.** The reason gap, drawn and judged.

On a real engagement all three ship; for demo or portfolio use, the report alone is enough.

## Why the buyer, and only the buyer

Three possible sources. Two are unusable.

**The CRM.** A Salesforce study of 24 companies found 50% of the data inaccurate. The recorded loss reason is typed in by the rep who lost the deal, and "price" tells you nothing you can act on.

**The sales team.** Reps are wrong about why they win and lose **60% of the time**, and the bias runs one way — towards reasons outside their control. Primary Intelligence, across 50,000 interviews, found reps blame price 48% of the time and buyers name it as the real primary factor only 23%.

**The buyer.** The only person who knows. B2B decisions turn on things the rep never sees.

## The interviewer

**Use a neutral interviewer.** 57% of companies who used an internal "neutral" employee admitted it was less effective because of bias. Third-party programmes report 70% satisfaction with feedback quality against 34% for internal ones.

Four options, in order of preference for an early-stage company:

| Interviewer | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Kernel AI interviewer** (default) | An asynchronous text interview on a link. Asks the eight questions in order with follow-ups, never sells, never defends. The closest thing to a third party most teams have. ## Interviews, not surveys

B2B decision makers accept an interview more than 30% of the time and a survey less than 5%. They give 20 to 40 minutes to a call and under 5 to a form. An AI interview keeps the interview shape: open questions and follow-ups, never a rating form.

## Interview requests

Sent by the founder, from their own email or WhatsApp: a thank-you, 15 minutes, "I won't try to change your mind", and the link. Most replies come after the second message, so send one follow-up on day 5. Incentives are optional for customers and reasonable for people who did not buy.

## Who to interview

Three groups. **Most programmes skip the third and it is usually the largest.**

| Group | Why |
|---|---|
| **Won** | Tells you what actually closed it |
| **Lost** | To a competitor, the status quo, or an internal alternative. Tells you who you really compete with |
| **No decision** | They decided nothing. At early stage this is most of the pipeline |

**Scaling for an early-stage company.** Big programmes run 50+ interviews a quarter. A seed company might have twelve closed deals in total. So:

- Review every closed deal on record, however few.
- Interview at least eight buyers. If there aren't eight, interview all of them and say so.
- Weight towards losses and no decisions.
- Interview within 90 days of the decision.

## Seal the recorded reasons first

Before any interview, write down why each deal was lost according to the company. Then nobody looks at it again until the interviews are finished. This is the pre-call log, deliverable 1. In the app, Kernel seals it and opens it only after the last interview.

**If nothing was written down** — no CRM, no sales team, pre-launch — capture the founder's own recollection instead, in writing, before the interviews. Label it "your assumption" rather than "recorded loss reason". The method still works. One person's memory is exactly the bias this exists to catch.

**Never skip this step.** Without it there is no comparison, and the comparison is the output.

## The interview

**Rules**
- Never defend. The moment you argue, the data stops.
- Never sell. This is not a win-back call.
- Ask what happened, in order, not what they thought of you.
- The most useful answers come after you stop talking.

**Questions**

1. Walk me through how this started. What made you look at all?
2. Who else was in the running? In what order did they appear?
3. Who else inside your company (or household, for B2C) had a say?
4. What was the shortlist, and how did it get to that?
5. What decided it in the end?
6. What nearly went the other way?
7. What would we have had to do differently?
8. **For a no decision:** what did you do instead, and what would have to change for you to look again?

Five and seven rarely give the same answer. Both go in the output.

## Coding

1. Code each buyer's stated primary reason.
2. Put it beside the recorded loss reason and count the disagreements.
3. Group deal outcomes into: won, lost to a competitor, lost to the status quo, lost to an internal alternative, no decision.
4. Count as fractions of the total. Never a percentage on fewer than 30 deals.

## The report's structure

1. **Deal outcomes** — one mark per deal, grouped, drawn not tabled. Draw the empty group too. A dashed slot where competitors should be says more than the sentence.
2. **Reason gap** — one mark per interview, struck where the buyer disagreed, solid where they agreed. Then the pairs.
3. **Buyer's stated reasons** — ranked by count, drawn as filled marks out of the total, one verbatim each. Then questions 5 and 7 side by side.
4. **Key finding** — in a black band.
5. **Limitations.**

The header carries the deal count, the interview count and the date. That is enough — do not add a paragraph explaining the method.

## Voice

- Plain and direct. Their words in quotes, yours outside them.
- No slogans, no balanced two-part phrases.
- Sentence case. British spelling. No em dashes.
- Never soften a loss reason to protect the team.

## Rules

- **Three deliverables: the log, the guide, the report.** All three on real engagements; the report alone for demo use.
- **The reason gap is the output.** If recorded and stated reasons agree everywhere, say so — that is a rare finding and worth stating.
- **No decision is a deal outcome.** Never fold it into "lost".
- **Draw the counts.** Marks, not tables.
- **Never a percentage under 30 deals.**
- **Read the source, never the snippet.** The transcript is the source, not the interviewer's summary. Where a buyer names a competitor, read that competitor's pricing and positioning pages before drawing any conclusion about why they won. Anything that cannot be traced to something read in full stays out.
- **Anonymise.** Role and company type, never names.
- **Never use a recorded loss reason as the answer.** It is the thing being tested.
