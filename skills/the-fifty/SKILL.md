# The Fifty

Fifty questions. Every competitor a buyer actually considers, including the status quo. Every cell carrying a source and a date, or an explicit reason there isn't one.

The output is not a summary of competitors. It is a standing competitive grid where a blank is impossible, because a blank cannot be told apart from a question nobody asked.

## Two deliverables

**1. The grid workbook.** The full fifty rows as a spreadsheet on the client's own drive — every cell with its value, source URL, capture date and status. This is the standing reference: the quarterly refresh happens in it, the Competitor monitor watches rows in it, and the client keeps it when the engagement ends. One sheet per category or one sheet total, plain formatting, no charts. In the app it downloads, and a read-only link can be shared.

**2. The report.** The competitive grid judged and drawn — the count-scale picture, coverage by category, one row blown up in full, the key finding.

The workbook is the product a buyer of this output actually uses week to week. The report is how it is presented and shown. On a real engagement both ship. For demo or portfolio use, the report alone is enough, though a public sample workbook is worth publishing.

## Allowed evidence

**Only what competitors publish.** Their pages, their pricing, their docs, their filings, their job ads, their app store listings, public review corpora.

**Nothing from the other outputs.** No customer interview quotes. No win-loss counts. No market sizing. Those are different documents with different evidence, and importing them here does three bad things: it makes the grid unusable unless the client has already done two other outputs, it blurs what the document is, and it lets a weak grid borrow a strong finding it did not earn.

If the grid does not contain a key finding, the grid is not finished. Go back to the cells.

The one exception is the Table-stakes read, which is a separate output whose whole job is to judge this grid. Judgment lives there, not here.

## Why this shape wins

Read directly from competitor sites in August 2026:

- The consultancies ship 3–5 competitors, or treat competitor research as an input to positioning and ship no competitive artifact at all.
- The software platforms ship depth, but they are built for a company with a sales team, a CI manager and dozens of competitors.

There is nothing in between: no sourced, standing grid for a company with six competitors and no sales team.

The strongest supporting evidence comes from a competitor's own survey of 250+ CI and PMM professionals: the number one thing they say would make them trust competitive output, at 89%, is verifiable sourced outputs traceable back to original content. 81% cannot tell how fresh their data is. The top failure, at 73%, is an answer that sounds confident with weak sources.

## Confirm competitors

In the app, Kernel proposes the competitor list: competitors named on the client's own pages, same-category app store listings where they apply, and suggestions checked against each company's own site. The client confirms competitors, adds the ones buyers mention, and describes the status quo behaviour. Four to eight columns is right, and the client's own column is added automatically.

## The six categories

| Category | Rows |
|---|---|
| Market | 6 |
| Product | 12 |
| Positioning | 4 |
| Marketing | 14 |
| Sales and CS | 7 |
| Company | 7 |

Marketing carries the most rows because it is the most observable from outside. Company carries the fewest answerable ones.

## The columns

Every real competitor, plus one status quo column for the buyer who buys nothing.

**Label the status quo column with the behaviour, not just the term.** "Status quo" alone does not tell a reader what the person does. Pair the term with the behaviour:

| Right | Wrong |
|---|---|
| Status quo: leaving it in the current account | Status quo |
| Status quo: carrying on with the spreadsheet | Doing nothing, null alternative |
| Status quo: asking a friend who does this | Informal alternative |

Then say in one sentence, in the output, who that person is. A description, not a finding: "the person who never signs up for anything and leaves the money in their current account." Do not justify the column with evidence from another output.

Fill the column properly. Its price is zero. Its outcome is not.

## Cell states

Every cell gets one of these. There is no sixth state and there are no blanks.

| State | Means |
|---|---|
| **Answered** | A fact, quoted as published, with a URL and a capture date |
| **Not published** | The company does this, but does not say what |
| **Not found** | Looked, couldn't find it. Different from the above |
| **Not applicable** | The question is not relevant to this competitor |
| **Internal data** | Only the client can answer. In the app these are listed under "Internal data (only you can answer)", and the answers stay private to the client |

Draw the states with the standard fill grammar: solid answered (green in the app), dashed for not published and not found, struck where a previously published fact has been withdrawn.

**Quote, never paraphrase.** Pricing, customer counts and headcounts are the three most-repeated and least-checked numbers in any deck.

## The rules

- **Two deliverables: the workbook and the report.** The workbook on real engagements always.
- **No silent blanks.** Ever.
- **Every cell carries a source and a date, or an explicit status.**
- **Read the source, never the snippet.** Fetch and read the actual page. A search-result summary is a lead, not evidence. This rule is the entire product.
- **Never present a competitor's claim as a fact.** "Says 0.49% flat" is a fact about what they say.
- **Do not rank.** The grid reports.

## The two rows that pay for the rest

Perceived strengths and perceived weaknesses, from public review corpora, with the pattern and its frequency. These are the rows the battlecard is built from. Review corpora are public, so they are allowed here — customer interviews the client ran are not. If there is no review corpus for a competitor, mark it not found rather than substituting your own opinion.

## Cadence

Full refresh quarterly. Weekly monitor on the volatile rows only — pricing, messaging, tagline, press, acquisitions, hiring. That weekly run is the Competitor monitor output, not this one.

## The report's structure

Header: `The Fifty · N rows · M competitors · <capture date>`.

**01 · Competitive grid** — the whole thing drawn at count scale, the coverage split, and one sentence describing the status quo column.

**02 · Coverage by category** — drawn, ordered most answerable to least.

**03 · Row N · <the row name>** — one row blown up with every cell, verbatim, sourced and dated. Pick the row that decides the most deals, usually price.

**04 · Key finding** — one sentence, black band, drawn only from the cells.

**05 · Limitations** — the standing limits.

## Writing rules

**No section subtitles unless they carry information the section does not.** A heading plus a drawn thing is usually enough. Most subtitles exist to fill space and end up as slogans.

**Never write a balanced two-part line.** These read as clever rather than true and they are the most common failure in this work:

- "Too small to read. Big enough to count."
- "The blanks cluster. That tells you what this market lets you see."
- "A grid that fills those in is guessing, and a competitor is entitled to have secrets."

All three are wrong. Replace with the plain fact or delete. The same goes for footer lines: no motto under the page.

Test every line: if it has a rhythm, or a turn, or would work on a poster, cut it.

**The key finding is a fact, not a formulation.** "The column with no website beat the others" is a riddle. "Four of the six publish a fee, and all four lead with it." is a key finding. If the reader has to decode it, rewrite it.

## Language

Standard product marketing terms on every label and heading. Never a label built from "who, what, which, where" phrasing.

| Use | Instead of |
|---|---|
| Competitive grid | The grid |
| Competition, competitors | Alternatives |
| Confirm competitors | Confirm the list |
| Status quo, with the behaviour beside it | Never starting, doing nothing, no-decision, inertia |
| Status quo behaviour | Name what non-buyers do |
| Internal data (only you can answer) | Fill your private cells |
| Answered, Not published, Not found, Not applicable, Internal data | They don't publish it, looked and couldn't find it, N/A, data unavailable |
| What they say | Their positioning, their claim |
| Captured on | As of, per our research |
| Key finding, Limitations | The finding, what can't be known |

## Standing rules for every output

- **No invented findings.**
- **No false zeroes.**
- **No competitor prices as structural facts.** Prices belong in this grid, not in the market report.
- **Name organisations, never individuals**, when the work is confidential.
- **"Not measured" is not an answer.**
- **Read the source, never the snippet.**
